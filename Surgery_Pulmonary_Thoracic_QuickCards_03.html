Pleural physiology#1
What normally keeps the lung expanded against the chest wall?
Show answer
Negative intrapleural pressure plus the mechanical coupling between visceral and parietal pleura.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: The lung naturally recoils inward while the chest wall tends outward; subatmospheric pleural pressure couples them.
Pleural physiology#2
What happens physiologically when air enters the pleural space?
Show answer
Pleural pressure moves toward atmospheric pressure, the normal transpulmonary pressure gradient is lost, and the lung recoils/collapses.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Pneumothorax is fundamentally loss of the pressure relationship that keeps the lung open.
Pneumothorax#3
Why are breath sounds decreased on the side of a pneumothorax?
Show answer
The affected lung is partially or completely collapsed and receives less ventilation.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Less moving air = less transmitted breath sound.
Pneumothorax#4
Why is a pneumothorax hyperresonant to percussion?
Show answer
Extra pleural air increases resonance.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Air = hyperresonance; pleural fluid/blood = dullness.
Pneumothorax#5
What is the usual initial imaging study for a stable patient with suspected pneumothorax?
Show answer
Chest radiograph.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: CXR can show a visceral pleural line with absent peripheral lung markings; CT is more sensitive but is not usually needed first in a straightforward stable case.
Pneumothorax#6
What CXR finding supports pneumothorax?
Show answer
A visible visceral pleural line with no lung markings peripheral to it.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: The lung has retracted away from the chest wall.
Pneumothorax#7
What does a deep sulcus sign suggest on a supine chest radiograph?
Show answer
Pneumothorax.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: In supine patients, pleural air can collect anteriorly/basally rather than at the apex, making the costophrenic angle unusually deep/lucent.
Tension pneumothorax#8
Why can tension pneumothorax cause hypotension?
Show answer
Increasing intrathoracic pressure compresses the venae cavae → decreased venous return/preload → decreased stroke volume/cardiac output.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: This is obstructive shock, not primarily blood loss.
Tension pneumothorax#9
Why can JVD occur in tension pneumothorax?
Show answer
High intrathoracic pressure impedes venous return, increasing upstream systemic venous pressure.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: JVD reflects obstructed right-heart filling, though it may be absent if the patient is hypovolemic.
Tension pneumothorax#10
Should you wait for a CXR before treating an unstable patient with a classic tension pneumothorax presentation?
Show answer
No. Immediate decompression is indicated based on the clinical diagnosis.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Imaging delay can permit cardiovascular collapse.
Tension pneumothorax#11
What is definitive pleural management after emergency decompression of tension pneumothorax?
Show answer
Tube thoracostomy/chest tube.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Emergency decompression relieves pressure; the chest tube provides ongoing evacuation and lung re-expansion.
Hemothorax#12
What is a hemothorax?
Show answer
Blood within the pleural space.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Pleural blood can compromise both circulating volume and lung expansion.
Hemothorax#13
What percussion finding favors hemothorax over pneumothorax?
Show answer
Dullness to percussion.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Fluid/blood dampens sound; air increases resonance.
Hemothorax#14
What is the typical initial imaging study in a stable patient with suspected hemothorax?
Show answer
Chest radiograph; bedside ultrasound/eFAST is also very useful in trauma.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Pleural fluid can blunt the costophrenic angle or layer; ultrasound rapidly detects pleural fluid at bedside.
Hemothorax#15
Why can a large hemothorax cause shock?
Show answer
Significant intrathoracic blood loss decreases circulating volume; a large collection can also impair ventilation.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: The chest can hide a major hemorrhage.
Hemothorax#16
What is the usual initial definitive drainage treatment for a significant traumatic hemothorax?
Show answer
Tube thoracostomy.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: The tube evacuates blood, helps re-expand the lung, and allows measurement of ongoing bleeding.
Hemothorax#17
Why does chest-tube output matter in hemothorax?
Show answer
The amount and persistence of bleeding help assess whether hemorrhage is ongoing and whether operative control may be needed.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: The tube is diagnostic as well as therapeutic.
Pleural effusion#18
Why does pleural effusion cause dyspnea?
Show answer
Pleural fluid compresses adjacent lung, reducing ventilated lung volume and increasing work of breathing.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Large effusions create restrictive mechanical impairment.
Pleural effusion#19
What physical exam findings suggest a pleural effusion?
Show answer
Decreased breath sounds, dullness to percussion, and reduced tactile fremitus over the fluid.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Fluid dampens sound/vibration transmission from the lung.
Pleural effusion#20
What imaging can rapidly confirm and characterize pleural fluid at bedside?
Show answer
Thoracic ultrasound.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Ultrasound is highly useful for detecting fluid and guiding thoracentesis.
Pleural effusion#21
What does blunting of the costophrenic angle on upright CXR suggest?
Show answer
Pleural fluid/effusion.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Fluid gravitates dependently and obscures the normally sharp costophrenic recess.
Pleural effusion#22
Why is ultrasound guidance useful for thoracentesis?
Show answer
It identifies the fluid pocket and nearby diaphragm/organs, improving success and reducing complications such as pneumothorax.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Know where the fluid actually is before placing the needle.
Pleural effusion#23
What is the purpose of diagnostic thoracentesis?
Show answer
Obtain pleural fluid to determine the cause of an unexplained effusion.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Fluid analysis helps distinguish transudative vs exudative processes and evaluate infection, malignancy, etc.
Pleural effusion#24
What is the physiologic difference between a transudative and exudative effusion?
Show answer
Transudates result mainly from systemic hydrostatic/oncotic pressure imbalance; exudates result from local pleural inflammation/increased permeability or impaired lymphatic drainage.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Think systemic pressure problem vs local pleural disease.
Pleural effusion#25
Why can CHF cause bilateral transudative pleural effusions?
Show answer
Elevated pulmonary/systemic hydrostatic pressures drive low-protein fluid across pleural capillaries.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Again: pump dysfunction → elevated filling pressures → fluid accumulation.
Empyema#26
What is an empyema?
Show answer
Infected purulent fluid within the pleural space.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: This is not just pneumonia; infection has entered the pleural compartment.
Empyema#27
Why are antibiotics alone sometimes insufficient for empyema?
Show answer
Purulent/loculated infected pleural fluid often requires source control with drainage.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Infected collections frequently need drainage plus antimicrobial therapy.
Empyema#28
What imaging is particularly useful for detecting septations/loculations in pleural infection?
Show answer
Thoracic ultrasound; CT can further define pleural anatomy and complications.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Loculations can prevent free drainage and affect procedural planning.
Empyema#29
What is the key management concept for empyema?
Show answer
Appropriate antibiotics plus pleural drainage/source control; surgery may be required for organized disease.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Treat both the organism and the infected space.
Chylothorax#30
What is chylothorax?
Show answer
Accumulation of lymphatic chyle in the pleural space due to thoracic duct disruption or obstruction.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: The thoracic duct transports lipid-rich lymph; injury or obstruction leaks it into the chest.
Chylothorax#31
What pleural-fluid appearance may suggest chylothorax?
Show answer
Milky fluid, especially after enteral fat intake, although appearance alone is not diagnostic.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: The uploaded source also notes that fluid may be clear if the patient has not been fed and milky when feeding, with increased lymphocytes.
Chylothorax#32
Why can prolonged chylothorax cause nutritional and immune problems?
Show answer
Chyle contains fats, proteins, electrolytes, and lymphocytes, so ongoing loss depletes nutrition and immune cells.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: It is more than a mechanical effusion.
Chylothorax#33
What laboratory feature confirms chylous pleural fluid?
Show answer
Elevated pleural-fluid triglycerides and/or chylomicrons, interpreted clinically.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Chylomicrons are particularly specific for true chyle.
Chest tube#34
What is the purpose of a chest tube in pneumothorax?
Show answer
Continuously evacuate pleural air so negative pleural pressure can be restored and the lung can re-expand.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Treat the abnormal pleural space, not the lung tissue itself.
Chest tube#35
What is the purpose of a chest tube in hemothorax/empyema?
Show answer
Drain blood or infected pleural fluid, promote lung re-expansion, and provide source control/monitoring.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: The indication determines what you expect to come out.
Chest tube#36
Why is the chest-tube drainage system kept below the level of the chest?
Show answer
Gravity helps drainage and reduces the chance of fluid flowing backward toward the pleural space.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Do not create a path for contaminated fluid to return.
Chest tube#37
What can continuous bubbling in the water-seal chamber suggest?
Show answer
An ongoing air leak, depending on the drainage system and clinical context.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Intermittent bubbling may occur with coughing early on; continuous bubbling prompts evaluation of patient and tubing/system.
Chest tube#38
Why should you assess the patient before focusing on the chest-tube device when something looks wrong?
Show answer
Clinical deterioration may represent recurrent pneumothorax, obstruction, dislodgement, bleeding, or another emergency requiring immediate patient-centered assessment.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Treat the patient, not the equipment.
Pulmonary nodule#39
What is a pulmonary nodule?
Show answer
A focal rounded pulmonary opacity, generally ≤3 cm; larger lesions are termed masses.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Size, morphology, growth, smoking history, and age influence malignancy risk.
Pulmonary nodule#40
What imaging modality best characterizes an indeterminate pulmonary nodule found on CXR?
Show answer
Chest CT.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: CT evaluates size, borders, calcification, density, location, and additional lesions.
Pulmonary nodule#41
Why are spiculated margins concerning?
Show answer
Irregular radiating edges increase suspicion for invasive malignancy compared with smooth benign-appearing margins.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Morphology changes pretest probability; it does not alone prove cancer.
Pulmonary nodule#42
Why is comparison with prior imaging important?
Show answer
Growth over time raises concern for malignancy, while long-term stability can support benignity depending on nodule type.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: A single snapshot cannot show biologic behavior.
Lung malignancy#43
What is the basic diagnostic sequence when lung cancer is suspected on imaging?
Show answer
Characterize/stage with appropriate imaging and obtain tissue diagnosis using the safest highest-yield approach based on lesion location and stage.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Cancer treatment depends on histology and stage, so tissue and staging matter.
Lung malignancy#44
Why can a central lung tumor cause post-obstructive pneumonia or atelectasis?
Show answer
Tumor obstructs a bronchus → impaired ventilation and secretion clearance distal to the blockage.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Mechanical airway obstruction creates collapse and infection risk.
Lung malignancy#45
Why can an apical/Pancoast tumor cause shoulder pain and neurologic symptoms?
Show answer
Apical extension can invade the brachial plexus and nearby sympathetic structures.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Anatomic location explains symptoms outside the lung.
Lung malignancy#46
What syndrome can occur when a thoracic malignancy compresses the superior vena cava?
Show answer
Superior vena cava syndrome.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Impaired venous drainage from the head/upper extremities causes facial/neck swelling, venous distention, and related symptoms.
Mediastinum#47
Why is a widened mediastinum on trauma CXR concerning?
Show answer
It can suggest major mediastinal vascular injury such as traumatic aortic injury, although the finding is not specific.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: In the right mechanism, it should trigger definitive vascular imaging in a stable patient.
Mediastinum#48
What imaging is commonly used to evaluate suspected acute aortic/major mediastinal vascular injury in a stable patient?
Show answer
CT angiography of the chest.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: CTA rapidly defines vascular anatomy and injury.
Mediastinum#49
What are the classic '4 Ts' of anterior mediastinal masses?
Show answer
Thymoma, teratoma/germ-cell tumor, thyroid mass, and 'terrible' lymphoma.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Anatomic compartment narrows the differential.
Perioperative pulmonary#50
Why does general anesthesia predispose to atelectasis?
Show answer
Reduced functional residual capacity, diaphragmatic changes, supine positioning, and altered ventilation promote dependent alveolar collapse.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Anesthesia changes lung mechanics even before postoperative pain is added.
Perioperative pulmonary#51
Why does postoperative pain increase pulmonary complication risk?
Show answer
Splinting and shallow breathing reduce tidal volumes, cough, and secretion clearance → atelectasis and pneumonia risk.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Good analgesia can improve ventilation, but excessive opioids can depress respiration.
Perioperative pulmonary#52
Why does early ambulation help prevent pulmonary complications?
Show answer
It improves lung expansion, ventilation, secretion clearance, and reduces immobility-related complications.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Movement is pulmonary therapy as well as DVT prevention.
Perioperative pulmonary#53
What is the first question when a postoperative patient suddenly becomes hypoxemic?
Show answer
Assess ABCs and rapidly consider immediately dangerous causes such as airway obstruction, hypoventilation, aspiration, pneumothorax, PE, pulmonary edema, or major atelectasis.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Do not anchor on one diagnosis; stabilize while localizing the physiology.
Perioperative pulmonary#54
Why can positive-pressure ventilation lower blood pressure?
Show answer
Increased intrathoracic pressure reduces venous return/preload and can lower cardiac output, especially in hypovolemia.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Ventilation affects circulation.
