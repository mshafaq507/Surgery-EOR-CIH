PERIOPERATIVE#1
Why can hypovolemia cause hypotension after surgery?
Show answer
Decreased intravascular volume → decreased venous return/preload → decreased stroke volume → decreased cardiac output → hypotension.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Think volume → preload → stroke volume → cardiac output.
PERIOPERATIVE#2
Why is urine output followed closely after surgery?
Show answer
It is a practical marker of renal perfusion and can signal hypovolemia, shock, obstruction, or acute kidney injury.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Low urine output is a finding, not a diagnosis. Check perfusion, Foley patency, and renal function.
PERIOPERATIVE#3
What adult urine output is commonly used as a minimum perfusion target?
Show answer
About ≥0.5 mL/kg/hr.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Persistent oliguria should trigger reassessment of hemodynamics, volume status, catheter patency, medications, and renal function.
PERIOPERATIVE#4
Why are postoperative patients at increased risk for DVT/PE?
Show answer
Virchow triad: venous stasis + endothelial injury + hypercoagulability.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Surgery and immobility strongly promote venous stasis; inflammation, malignancy, and vascular injury can add risk.
PERIOPERATIVE#5
Why can atelectasis occur after surgery?
Show answer
Anesthesia, pain, shallow breathing, immobility, and retained secretions reduce alveolar expansion and promote collapse.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Think reduced lung expansion, especially after abdominal/thoracic surgery.
PERIOPERATIVE#6
Why does early ambulation help after surgery?
Show answer
It reduces venous stasis and improves ventilation, secretion clearance, and functional recovery.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: It helps both thromboembolic and pulmonary recovery.
SHOCK#7
What is shock physiologically?
Show answer
Failure of the circulation to deliver enough oxygenated blood to meet tissue metabolic needs.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: The key endpoint is inadequate tissue perfusion/oxygen delivery.
SHOCK#8
What determines cardiac output?
Show answer
Cardiac output = heart rate × stroke volume.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Stroke volume is influenced by preload, contractility, and afterload.
SHOCK#9
What happens to preload in hemorrhagic shock?
Show answer
Preload decreases because blood volume and venous return fall.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Decreased preload → decreased stroke volume → decreased cardiac output.
SHOCK#10
Why does hemorrhagic shock cause tachycardia?
Show answer
Sympathetic activation increases heart rate to compensate for falling stroke volume and maintain cardiac output.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: CO = HR × SV.
SHOCK#11
What is the main hemodynamic problem in cardiogenic shock?
Show answer
Pump failure → inadequate stroke volume and cardiac output despite adequate or increased filling volume.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: This is primarily a pump problem, not simply a volume problem.
SHOCK#12
Why can tension pneumothorax cause obstructive shock?
Show answer
Rising intrathoracic pressure compresses great veins → decreased venous return → decreased preload → decreased cardiac output.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: The heart cannot fill adequately because venous return is mechanically obstructed.
SHOCK#13
Why can lactate rise in shock?
Show answer
Inadequate oxygen delivery can increase anaerobic metabolism; impaired clearance can also contribute.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Use lactate as a marker of physiologic stress/hypoperfusion in context.
FLUIDS / BLOOD#14
Why can isotonic crystalloid increase preload?
Show answer
It expands extracellular/intravascular volume and can increase venous return.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Fluids help when inadequate effective circulating volume is limiting cardiac output.
FLUIDS / BLOOD#15
Why can too much IV fluid worsen pulmonary edema in heart failure?
Show answer
A failing pump may not handle increased preload, raising pulmonary venous/capillary hydrostatic pressure and driving fluid into the lungs.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Always ask: does this patient need volume, or can the pump not handle more volume?
FLUIDS / BLOOD#16
What is the main physiologic purpose of packed RBC transfusion?
Show answer
To increase red-cell mass and blood oxygen-carrying capacity.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Hemoglobin carries oxygen; PRBCs are not merely a volume intervention.
FLUIDS / BLOOD#17
A patient develops concerning symptoms during a transfusion. What is the immediate principle?
Show answer
Stop the transfusion and evaluate for a transfusion reaction while supporting ABCs.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Do not continue the blood while deciding which reaction is occurring.
FLUIDS / BLOOD#18
Why can massive transfusion cause hypocalcemia?
Show answer
Citrate in transfused blood products binds ionized calcium.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Low calcium can impair myocardial function and coagulation.
GI / ABDOMEN#19
Why does peritoneal irritation cause guarding and rebound tenderness?
Show answer
Parietal peritoneal inflammation activates somatic pain pathways and reflex abdominal-wall contraction.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Parietal peritoneal pain is localized and worsens with movement.
GI / ABDOMEN#20
Why can bowel obstruction cause vomiting and abdominal distention?
Show answer
Fluid, gas, and intestinal contents accumulate proximal to the obstruction because forward transit is blocked.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: The level of obstruction affects how early vomiting versus distention appears.
GI / ABDOMEN#21
Why can bowel obstruction cause hypovolemia without external bleeding?
Show answer
Vomiting plus sequestration of fluid into bowel/interstitial spaces reduces effective circulating volume.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Think GI loss + third spacing.
GI / ABDOMEN#22
Why is strangulated bowel obstruction dangerous?
Show answer
Compromised bowel blood flow can cause ischemia, necrosis, perforation, sepsis, and shock.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: An obstruction becomes a vascular emergency when perfusion is threatened.
GI / ABDOMEN#23
What is the usual initial imaging test for suspected acute cholecystitis?
Show answer
Right-upper-quadrant ultrasound.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Know both the first test and the findings you expect.
GI / ABDOMEN#24
What ultrasound findings support acute cholecystitis?
Show answer
Gallstones with supportive findings such as wall thickening, pericholecystic fluid, distention, or a sonographic Murphy sign.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Presentation → initial imaging → classic findings → management.
GI / ABDOMEN#25
Why can ileus occur after abdominal surgery?
Show answer
Surgical manipulation, inflammation, sympathetic activity, opioids, and electrolyte abnormalities can suppress coordinated intestinal motility.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Ileus is impaired motility without a mechanical transition point.
PULMONARY / THORACIC#26
Why are breath sounds decreased with pneumothorax?
Show answer
Pleural air separates the lung from the chest wall and allows partial/complete lung collapse, reducing ventilation on that side.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: The normal negative-pressure relationship keeping the lung expanded is disrupted.
PULMONARY / THORACIC#27
Why is pneumothorax often hyperresonant to percussion?
Show answer
Excess pleural air produces a more resonant percussion note.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Pleural blood/fluid instead tends to cause dullness.
PULMONARY / THORACIC#28
Why can tension pneumothorax cause hypotension?
Show answer
Increasing intrathoracic pressure impairs venous return → decreased preload → decreased cardiac output.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: This is obstructive shock physiology.
PULMONARY / THORACIC#29
Why should an unstable tension pneumothorax be treated before waiting for imaging?
Show answer
It is a life-threatening clinical diagnosis; delaying decompression can allow cardiovascular collapse.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Unstable classic presentation → decompress first.
PULMONARY / THORACIC#30
What does a chest tube accomplish in pneumothorax?
Show answer
It evacuates pleural air and permits lung re-expansion as abnormal pleural pressure resolves.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Treat the pleural-space problem.
PULMONARY / THORACIC#31
Why can a large hemothorax cause shock?
Show answer
Blood loss into the thorax reduces circulating volume and may also impair ventilation and venous return.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: It can create hemorrhagic plus mechanical cardiopulmonary compromise.
PULMONARY / THORACIC#32
What is thoracentesis used for?
Show answer
Diagnostic sampling and/or therapeutic drainage of pleural fluid.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: The Surgery topic list specifically includes thoracentesis.
RENAL / GU#33
Why can urinary retention occur after surgery?
Show answer
Anesthesia, opioids, pain, immobility, sympathetic activity, and underlying outlet obstruction can impair bladder emptying.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Distinguish retention from genuinely low urine production.
RENAL / GU#34
A postop patient has little urine in the Foley bag. What simple problem should you check before assuming AKI?
Show answer
Check the catheter and tubing for kinking, obstruction, displacement, or drainage problems.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Make sure urine can physically leave the bladder.
RENAL / GU#35
Why does prerenal AKI occur with hypovolemia?
Show answer
Reduced effective circulating volume lowers renal blood flow and glomerular perfusion.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Initially, the kidney may be structurally intact; the primary problem is perfusion.
RENAL / GU#36
Why can nephrolithiasis cause hydronephrosis?
Show answer
A stone obstructs urine flow, increasing pressure proximal to the obstruction and dilating the collecting system.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Obstruction can threaten renal function, especially when infected, bilateral, or in a solitary kidney.
RENAL / GU#37
Why is testicular torsion a surgical emergency?
Show answer
Twisting of the spermatic cord compromises testicular blood flow, causing ischemia and eventual infarction.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Time-sensitive detorsion/orchiopexy is required when torsion is suspected.
WOUNDS / SKIN#38
What is the core treatment principle for a drainable abscess?
Show answer
Source control with incision and drainage when appropriate.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Antibiotics alone may not resolve a walled-off purulent collection.
WOUNDS / SKIN#39
Why does cellulitis cause erythema, warmth, swelling, and tenderness?
Show answer
Inflammation causes vasodilation, increased vascular permeability, and immune-cell recruitment.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: The physical findings reflect the local inflammatory response.
WOUNDS / SKIN#40
Why do pressure injuries develop over bony prominences?
Show answer
Prolonged pressure can exceed capillary perfusion pressure, producing local ischemia and tissue injury.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Immobility, shear, moisture, poor nutrition, and poor perfusion increase risk.
AIRWAY / ANESTHESIA#41
Why is preoxygenation performed before endotracheal intubation?
Show answer
It increases oxygen reserves and delays desaturation during the apneic period.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: The goal is to increase safe apnea time.
AIRWAY / ANESTHESIA#42
What is the most reliable routine method to confirm tracheal tube placement?
Show answer
Continuous waveform capnography showing exhaled CO₂, interpreted with the clinical picture.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Chest rise and auscultation help assess position but are less reliable for confirming tracheal placement.
AIRWAY / ANESTHESIA#43
Why can positive-pressure ventilation decrease blood pressure?
Show answer
Higher intrathoracic pressure can reduce venous return → decreased preload → decreased cardiac output, especially in hypovolemia.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Intubation/ventilation can expose hemodynamic instability.
AIRWAY / ANESTHESIA#44
Why must sedation/induction occur before neuromuscular paralysis in RSI?
Show answer
Paralytics cause immobility but do not provide unconsciousness, amnesia, or analgesia.
WHY / HIGH-YIELD: Paralysis is not sedation.
