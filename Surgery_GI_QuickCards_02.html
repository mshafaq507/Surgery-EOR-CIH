Rotation method: answer OUT LOUD before tapping. For diagnostic cards, say: diagnosis → initial test → expected finding → treatment. For physiology cards, explain the mechanism as a chain.
Appendicitis#1
What causes the pain of appendicitis to migrate from periumbilical to the RLQ?
Show answer
Early visceral afferent pain is referred to the periumbilical region; later parietal peritoneal irritation localizes pain to the RLQ.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Visceral pain is vague/midline; parietal peritoneum has somatic innervation and produces sharp localized pain.
Appendicitis#2
What is the preferred initial imaging for suspected appendicitis in a nonpregnant adult when imaging is needed?
Show answer
CT abdomen/pelvis, typically with IV contrast.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: CT can show a dilated inflamed appendix, wall thickening/enhancement, periappendiceal fat stranding, fluid, abscess, or perforation.
Appendicitis#3
What imaging is generally preferred first for suspected appendicitis in pregnancy?
Show answer
Graded-compression ultrasound; MRI is useful if ultrasound is nondiagnostic.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Avoid ionizing radiation when possible while still obtaining timely diagnosis.
Appendicitis#4
What is the definitive treatment for uncomplicated acute appendicitis in the usual surgical approach?
Show answer
Appendectomy, with perioperative antibiotics covering enteric gram-negative organisms and anaerobes.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Source control removes the inflamed appendix; complicated disease may require broader source-control planning.
Appendicitis#5
Why can perforated appendicitis cause sepsis?
Show answer
Loss of appendiceal wall integrity spills bacteria/inflammatory contents into the peritoneum, causing peritonitis or abscess and potentially systemic infection.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Perforation converts a localized process into an intra-abdominal source-control problem.
Bowel obstruction#6
What are the classic symptoms of small-bowel obstruction?
Show answer
Crampy abdominal pain, vomiting, distention, and obstipation/constipation.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Obstruction causes proximal accumulation of gas, fluid, and intestinal contents.
Bowel obstruction#7
What is the most useful imaging study for defining the site/cause and complications of suspected SBO in an adult?
Show answer
CT abdomen/pelvis with IV contrast in most patients.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: CT can identify a transition point and signs of ischemia, closed-loop obstruction, perforation, or another cause.
Bowel obstruction#8
What does a transition point on CT mean?
Show answer
A change from dilated bowel proximally to decompressed bowel distally, localizing mechanical obstruction.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Mechanical obstruction has a physical point where forward flow is blocked.
Bowel obstruction#9
Why can SBO cause prerenal AKI?
Show answer
Vomiting and third spacing reduce effective circulating volume → reduced renal perfusion.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: GI pathology can become a hemodynamic/renal problem.
Bowel obstruction#10
What is initial management of uncomplicated SBO without peritonitis or ischemia?
Show answer
NPO, IV fluids/electrolyte correction, analgesia/antiemetics, surgical evaluation, and NG decompression when indicated.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: You stabilize and decompress while determining whether the obstruction will resolve or needs operative source correction.
Bowel obstruction#11
What findings make bowel obstruction an operative emergency?
Show answer
Peritonitis, perforation, strangulation/ischemia, clinical deterioration, or certain closed-loop/complete obstructions.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Threatened bowel perfusion means waiting risks necrosis and perforation.
Bowel obstruction#12
Why does strangulation first impair venous outflow before arterial inflow?
Show answer
Thin-walled veins are compressed more easily, causing congestion/edema; rising pressure can then compromise arterial perfusion.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Venous obstruction → edema → worsening pressure → arterial ischemia → necrosis.
Ileus#13
How does postoperative ileus differ physiologically from mechanical obstruction?
Show answer
Ileus is diffuse failure of coordinated intestinal motility without a mechanical blockage.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: No physical transition point is required; surgery, opioids, inflammation, and electrolytes can suppress motility.
Ileus#14
What imaging pattern favors ileus over mechanical SBO?
Show answer
Diffuse bowel dilation involving small and often large bowel without a discrete transition point.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Mechanical SBO more often has proximal dilation with distal decompression.
Ileus#15
How is uncomplicated postoperative ileus managed?
Show answer
Supportive care: correct fluids/electrolytes, minimize opioids, mobilize, treat underlying causes, and decompress if needed.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Restore physiology and remove factors suppressing motility rather than operating on a nonexistent blockage.
Gallbladder#16
RUQ pain after fatty meals without fever or persistent inflammation suggests what?
Show answer
Biliary colic from transient cystic duct obstruction, usually due to gallstones.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Temporary obstruction causes pain; persistent obstruction with inflammation produces acute cholecystitis.
Gallbladder#17
RUQ pain, fever, leukocytosis, and Murphy sign suggest what diagnosis?
Show answer
Acute cholecystitis.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Persistent cystic duct obstruction triggers gallbladder inflammation.
Gallbladder#18
What is the initial imaging test for suspected acute cholecystitis?
Show answer
RUQ ultrasound.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: It detects stones and inflammatory findings without radiation.
Gallbladder#19
What ultrasound findings support acute cholecystitis?
Show answer
Gallstones, wall thickening, distention, pericholecystic fluid, and/or sonographic Murphy sign.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: The key is obstruction plus inflammatory change.
Gallbladder#20
If ultrasound is equivocal but acute cholecystitis remains strongly suspected, what functional test can help?
Show answer
HIDA scan.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Failure of gallbladder visualization suggests cystic duct obstruction.
Gallbladder#21
What is typical definitive treatment for acute calculous cholecystitis in an operative candidate?
Show answer
Early laparoscopic cholecystectomy plus supportive care and antibiotics when indicated.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Removing the diseased gallbladder provides source control and prevents recurrence.
Gallbladder#22
How does choledocholithiasis differ from simple cholelithiasis?
Show answer
Choledocholithiasis is a stone in the common bile duct, often causing cholestatic labs and ductal dilation ± jaundice.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Location matters: CBD obstruction affects bile drainage from the liver.
Gallbladder#23
What procedure can both diagnose and treat obstructing common bile duct stones when intervention is needed?
Show answer
ERCP.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: ERCP can access the biliary tree for sphincterotomy and stone extraction; it is therapeutic, not merely imaging.
Cholangitis#24
What is Charcot triad?
Show answer
Fever + RUQ pain + jaundice.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: It suggests ascending cholangitis from infected biliary obstruction.
Cholangitis#25
Why is ascending cholangitis dangerous?
Show answer
Obstruction plus infection raises biliary pressure and can promote bacteremia/sepsis.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Antibiotics alone may fail without relieving the obstruction.
Cholangitis#26
What is the key treatment principle in severe/acute ascending cholangitis?
Show answer
IV antibiotics, resuscitation, and urgent biliary decompression, commonly by ERCP.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: This is infection plus obstruction: treat both.
Pancreatitis#27
What two laboratory/imaging criteria can establish acute pancreatitis when combined with typical pain?
Show answer
Serum lipase ≥3× upper limit of normal and/or characteristic imaging; diagnosis generally requires 2 of 3: typical pain, enzyme elevation, imaging.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Do not order CT just to prove every straightforward case.
Pancreatitis#28
Why is lipase generally preferred over amylase for acute pancreatitis?
Show answer
Lipase is more pancreas-specific and remains elevated longer.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Use the test with better diagnostic utility.
Pancreatitis#29
When is CT especially useful in acute pancreatitis?
Show answer
When diagnosis is uncertain or complications/severe disease are suspected, particularly if the course is not improving.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: CT assesses necrosis, collections, and alternative diagnoses; uncomplicated classic pancreatitis often does not need immediate CT.
Pancreatitis#30
Why can severe pancreatitis cause hypotension?
Show answer
Inflammation increases capillary permeability and third spacing, while vomiting/poor intake add volume loss → reduced effective circulating volume.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Pancreatic inflammation can become systemic circulatory dysfunction.
Pancreatitis#31
What are the core initial treatments for acute pancreatitis?
Show answer
IV fluid resuscitation as clinically appropriate, analgesia, antiemetics, and early enteral/oral nutrition as tolerated; treat the cause.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Support perfusion and nutrition while addressing gallstones, alcohol, hypertriglyceridemia, etc.
Pancreatitis#32
Why are prophylactic antibiotics not routinely given for sterile pancreatitis/sterile necrosis?
Show answer
Inflammation/necrosis alone is not bacterial infection.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Antibiotics are for suspected/confirmed infection or another infectious indication.
Diverticulitis#33
What presentation is classic for acute diverticulitis?
Show answer
LLQ abdominal pain with tenderness, often fever and leukocytosis.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Inflamed diverticula commonly involve the sigmoid colon.
Diverticulitis#34
What imaging is typically used to evaluate suspected complicated or uncertain diverticulitis?
Show answer
CT abdomen/pelvis with IV contrast.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: CT shows bowel-wall thickening, pericolic fat stranding, abscess, perforation, fistula, or obstruction.
Diverticulitis#35
Why is colonoscopy generally avoided during an acute severe diverticulitis episode?
Show answer
Instrumentation/insufflation may increase perforation risk in acutely inflamed bowel.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Colonic evaluation is performed after the acute inflammation resolves when indicated.
Diverticulitis#36
What changes management when diverticulitis has a large abscess, free perforation, diffuse peritonitis, fistula, or obstruction?
Show answer
It becomes complicated disease requiring surgical consultation and source-control planning; drainage or surgery may be needed.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Complications mean inflammation has extended beyond a simple localized process.
GI bleed#37
What is the first priority in a patient with major GI bleeding and hemodynamic instability?
Show answer
ABCs and hemodynamic resuscitation before definitive endoscopic diagnosis/treatment.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: A bleeding patient can die from shock before the exact lesion matters.
GI bleed#38
What commonly distinguishes upper from lower GI bleeding clinically?
Show answer
Hematemesis/melena usually suggests upper GI bleeding; hematochezia usually suggests lower bleeding but can occur with brisk upper GI hemorrhage.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Transit time and bleeding rate affect stool appearance.
GI bleed#39
What is the main diagnostic/therapeutic procedure for suspected significant upper GI bleeding after stabilization?
Show answer
Upper endoscopy (EGD).
WHY / PIMP PEARL: EGD visualizes the source and can provide endoscopic hemostasis.
GI bleed#40
Why can BUN rise disproportionately in upper GI bleeding?
Show answer
Digested blood protein increases urea production, and hypovolemia can increase urea reabsorption.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: The BUN/creatinine pattern can support, but not prove, an upper source.
GI bleed#41
What is the role of colonoscopy in lower GI bleeding?
Show answer
After appropriate stabilization/preparation, it can identify and sometimes treat a colonic bleeding source.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Resuscitation comes before procedural localization in unstable hemorrhage.
Peritonitis#42
What physical finding strongly suggests peritoneal inflammation?
Show answer
Involuntary guarding/rigidity and pain with movement or rebound/percussion tenderness.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Parietal peritoneal irritation activates somatic nerves and reflex muscle contraction.
Peritonitis#43
Why is free intraperitoneal air concerning?
Show answer
It suggests perforation of a hollow viscus until proven otherwise in the appropriate clinical context.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Perforation spills gas and contaminated contents into the peritoneal cavity and often requires urgent source control.
Toxic megacolon#44
What is toxic megacolon?
Show answer
Acute toxic systemic illness with nonobstructive colonic dilation, classically associated with severe colitis.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Severe inflammation impairs colonic motility and can progress to perforation/shock.
Toxic megacolon#45
Why should antidiarrheals and drugs that markedly slow motility generally be avoided in toxic megacolon?
Show answer
Further slowing an already dilated, inflamed colon can worsen distention and risk perforation.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: The colon needs decompression/recovery, not additional pharmacologic stasis.
Toxic megacolon#46
When does toxic megacolon require urgent surgery?
Show answer
Perforation, uncontrolled bleeding, worsening shock/toxicity, or failure of medical therapy.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Operate when the colon is failing or complications threaten life.
Hernia#47
What makes an incarcerated hernia different from a strangulated hernia?
Show answer
Incarcerated = trapped/nonreducible contents; strangulated = compromised blood supply.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Strangulation adds ischemia and makes the situation emergent.
Hernia#48
Why is a strangulated hernia a surgical emergency?
Show answer
Vascular compromise can cause bowel ischemia, necrosis, perforation, and sepsis.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Again: obstruction + impaired perfusion = threatened bowel.
Anorectal#49
Severe pain with defecation and bright-red blood on toilet paper most strongly suggests what common anorectal lesion?
Show answer
Anal fissure.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: A tear in the anoderm causes intense pain, often associated with constipation/hard stool.
Anorectal#50
Painless bright-red rectal bleeding is more typical of internal or external hemorrhoids?
Show answer
Internal hemorrhoids.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Internal hemorrhoids arise above the dentate line where visceral innervation makes them less pain-sensitive.
Anorectal#51
Why is a thrombosed external hemorrhoid painful?
Show answer
It is below the dentate line in somatically innervated tissue, and acute thrombosis stretches/inflames the tissue.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Dentate-line anatomy explains the pain difference.
Nutrition / tubes#52
Why is enteral nutrition preferred over parenteral nutrition when the GI tract is functional?
Show answer
It preserves gut integrity/function and generally has fewer catheter/metabolic complications than parenteral nutrition.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: If the gut works, use it.
Nutrition / tubes#53
What is the basic purpose of an NG tube in bowel obstruction?
Show answer
Gastric/proximal GI decompression to reduce vomiting, distention, and aspiration risk.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: It treats accumulation proximal to obstruction; it does not remove the mechanical cause.
Nutrition / tubes#54
What is a PEG tube primarily used for?
Show answer
Longer-term enteral access when the GI tract works but oral intake is inadequate or unsafe.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: PEG bypasses the mouth/esophagus for feeding but still uses the GI tract.
Ostomy#55
What should a healthy newly created stoma generally look like?
Show answer
Pink to red and moist.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Dusky, gray, black, or progressively ischemic appearance raises concern for impaired perfusion.
Ostomy#56
Why can a high-output ileostomy cause dehydration and electrolyte problems?
Show answer
The ileum can lose large amounts of sodium- and water-rich intestinal fluid before colonic reabsorption occurs.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: No colon means less opportunity to reclaim water/electrolytes.
Perioperative GI#57
Why is aspiration risk important before anesthesia?
Show answer
Loss of protective airway reflexes can allow gastric contents to enter the lungs, causing chemical pneumonitis and/or infection.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Fasting and airway strategy are about protecting the lungs from gastric contents.
Perioperative GI#58
Why can opioids worsen postoperative constipation and ileus?
Show answer
μ-opioid receptor activation reduces GI propulsive motility and increases sphincter tone.
WHY / PIMP PEARL: Analgesia can directly suppress bowel function.
